# Credibility: people, proof, and identity

Observations checked September 9, 2026. [CONTEXT.md](CONTEXT.md) defines evidence vocabulary. The site is a primary source for what Found42 publishes; publication alone is not independent verification of career history, client results, or endorsement.

## Founder and business identity

The About page identifies Richard Achée as founder and CEO. It describes more than two decades of enterprise experience across three continents, sponsorship of the Cameyo and Neverware acquisitions at Google, startup business development, board advisory and fractional GTM work, and a network of founders, fractional executives, domain experts, and strategic partners. These statements are published biography, not an audited resume. The page does not identify a staffed delivery team or the individuals behind the introductory article's broader Google/Amazon veteran claim. [Sources: About](https://www.found42.com/about), [introduction](https://www.found42.com/blog/blog-post-title-two-cep4t).

The footer presents the legal/business name Found42 LLC and a Manhattan address. Use Found42 for the brand and preserve the published full name where organizational identity matters. Blog bylines render Richard Achee without the accent; the biography uses Achée. Preserve exact source attribution when quoting while using the biography's spelling for new editorial prose. [Sources: About](https://www.found42.com/about), [blog](https://www.found42.com/blog).

The workshop acquisition case and personality profiles are explicitly instructional material, not customer proof. ## Brand assets

The public [media-assets page](https://www.found42.com/mediaassets) exposes three images: a C-Level AI banner graphic, a transparent Found42 logo, and the same founder portrait used on About. This is useful source discovery beyond the earlier homepage-only asset inventory. It does not establish an official vector master, color specification, complete brand guide, or unrestricted reuse license.

The [live homepage](https://www.found42.com/) uses black/red branding, large C-Level AI artwork, and a much smaller header logo. The observed mobile opening gives substantially more space to the program artwork than to the business mark. Recommendation: make the Found42 identity legible and make the relationship between the company and C-Level AI explicit. Preserve the earlier raster color sample as provisional rather than promoting it to an official brand swatch.

Some media-assets image descriptions are filenames rather than useful editorial alternatives. The homepage combines descriptive image alternatives with empty alternatives. The correct treatment depends on whether each image communicates information or is decorative; an empty alternative is not automatically a defect. [Sources: media assets](https://www.found42.com/mediaassets), [homepage](https://www.found42.com/).

## Credibility risks outside the main navigation

The public `/member-site-homepage-2` route contains generic template copy, unrelated lifestyle/product imagery, “Members Only” content, and event placeholders with self-targeting links. `/new-page-2` returns HTTP 200 but has no meaningful extracted main content. These pages do not establish a membership product, event program, or store. Their publication status needs review before migration or promotion. [Sources: member route](https://www.found42.com/member-site-homepage-2), [empty route](https://www.found42.com/new-page-2).

The Terms page's drafting-style introduction and incorrect policy link also weaken trust; the concrete details are in [Inquiries](../inquiries/AUDIT.md). The redesign should audit discoverable routes as well as navigation, since a page can be public without being a deliberate offer.
