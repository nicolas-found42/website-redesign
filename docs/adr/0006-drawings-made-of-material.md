# Drawings made of material, placed by hand

**Status:** accepted (2026-09-28); its opening, Workshops, Executives, Contributors and Builders drawings, and its print and colour prescriptions, are superseded by [ADR 0008](0008-text-led-opening-and-solid-drawings.md)

> The September 29 review changed several of these drawings; see
> [ADR 0008](0008-text-led-opening-and-solid-drawings.md). The homepage opening
> no longer carries the plait; Workshops, Executives, Contributors and Builders
> are redrawn as it describes; and the dots, stripes and rules below, with every
> dashed detail, are gone from all drawings. Workflows, Automations and the
> review scene keep the objects described here, without patterns.

Every drawing on the site — the opening drawing, the three service drawings, the
three audience scenes, the playbook's review scene and the industry pages'
portrait — was redrawn from nothing. None of them is nodes joined by lines any
more. Each is one object made of paper, card and ink that shows its content by
how the material behaves:

| Drawing          | Object                 | What carries the flow                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ---------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Opening (master) | A plait                | People, workflows and what the business knows are three printed strands plaited together; a red band tied round the plait is human direction, and the plait leaves as one strand that ends in a tag, _Practical AI at work_, with two blank tags as the unnamed outputs. |
| Workshops        | A folded loop          | One strip, mitred at its corners: the team's real work runs into the red side (group review), where live guided practice is tucked in, comes back as a reusable skill, is applied afterwards and is tucked under the work it started from.                               |
| Workflows        | A concertina           | Brief, design and build, and test and review are the first three panels, then the red panel (the team deploying it) and the ink panel (review in context). That panel's tail is a stitched ribbon threaded back behind the panel it refits.                              |
| Automations      | A stamp                | Repetitive work is a stack of sheets, system handoffs a strip fed through slots, human review a sheet set aside; the red stamp (human direction) comes down and the usable output is the stamped sheet, with its two unnamed copies.                                     |
| Executives       | A roller               | The operating problem arrives crumpled, is fed through the tailored skill, and comes out flat as a brief laid on an illustrative operating view; a red clip is executive direction, and the view feeds back to the problem.                                              |
| Contributors     | Ribbons through a ring | Four role tabs, each with its own ribbon and buckle (the skill), gathered through one red ring: the human in the loop.                                                                                                                                                   |
| Builders         | A stair                | A learner climbs test, troubleshoot and anticipate failures, each block sealed by a reviewer, to the workflow in use, joined by a three-link chain.                                                                                                                      |
| Review           | Gates                  | A draft is fed down through three sealed gates, past a red band (human review), to an ink tag: it reaches the business.                                                                                                                                                  |

What stays exactly as it was is recorded in
`tests/fixtures/diagram-inventory.json`, generated by
`scripts/diagram-inventory.mjs` from the deployed commit's own modules, and
checked by `tests/diagrams.spec.ts`: every word, its kind and its beat, every
connection (including both return loops and the unnamed outputs), every mark on
a person, a check or a result, each drawing's accessible description and
caption, and the landscape/portrait differences. The colours are the site's
existing tokens — ink, paper and its warm and sunk tones, the strong line, muted
grey, red and deep red — and keep their meanings: red is what is human (plus
the reviewers' seals, as before), ink is the result, and in the four schematic
drawings the first three nodes keep one print each — dots, stripes and rules.

## What this changes in the earlier decisions

This supersedes the visual language of [ADR 0002](0002-working-system-drawing.md)
(routes at fixed angles, ringed markers, travelling signals, draw-on) and of
[ADR 0003](0003-audience-scenes.md) (ink routes, ringed markers, monospaced
annotations), and the sentence of [ADR 0005](0005-approachable-visual-voice.md)
that kept "their routed-node language". Their architecture stands: the still
composition is the resting state, every composition is authored separately for
landscape and portrait, the live engines take their compositions as arguments,
and the review scene has one composition.

- **Content and artwork are separate modules.** `src/schematic.ts` and
  `src/audiences.ts` now hold only words, kinds, beats and connections.
  `src/art/*` holds one bespoke drawing per composition, built from a small kit
  (`src/art/kit.ts`: strips, folds, tags, seals, figures and six prints). A
  drawing is free to be any object at all, because nothing about the content
  assumes a shape.
- **A part is the unit of a drawing.** Each piece is an outer group that nothing
  moves, carrying `data-beat`, `data-links`, `data-nodes` and `data-marks`, and
  an inner body that is the only thing an entrance touches. This keeps ADR
  0002's invariant — nothing that animates may also position — and lets a test
  read what a drawing claims to show without knowing how it looks.
- **Pieces are placed, not drawn.** An entrance is a Web Animation on a part's
  body with `fill: "backwards"`: a strand slides in, a sheet drops, a fold opens,
  a tag swings, a stamp comes down, in the drawing's beats, and then everything
  is still. Because the resting state is the element's own style, cancelling an
  entrance _is_ settling it; there is no draw state to release. Travelling
  signals, `stroke-dasharray` draws and the per-drawing signal loop are gone,
  and nothing loops. The only loop left is pointer depth, which is pausable as
  before.
- **Origins are in drawing units.** Bodies use `transform-box: view-box` with a
  pixel `transform-origin` in the drawing's own coordinates, so a fold opens at
  its hinge and a tag swings on its hole at every size. A test checks that it
  resolves as written in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit.
- **Words are real text over the artwork.** Labels are HTML set by share of the
  field, never below 13px, with a paper halo only where they sit on paper. They
  travel to their new places when a service drawing changes, while the old
  object is lifted off and the new one is laid.
- **The opening spread has two columns.** The headline, lead, actions and the
  participant's words take one column and the drawing the other; nothing is set
  over the drawing, so it can be covered at no width. Between 861px and 1179px
  the opening drawing takes its portrait composition beside the words. A test
  measures every text box against the drawing at fifteen widths.

Rejected:

- **Canvas, WebGL or Wasm rendering.** The drawings are still pictures that are
  assembled once; nothing in them needs a GPU or a simulation. Rendering them to
  a canvas would give up prerendered no-script stills and real text, and add 14
  to 150 KB gzipped of runtime (measurements in
  `research/diagram-redesign-2026-09-28.md`). GitHub Pages sends no
  cross-origin-isolation headers, so threaded Wasm would also need a workaround.
- **Keeping the node-and-route engine and restyling it.** The brief asked for a
  new concept, not a new skin; a restyled route is still a line between boxes.
- **The paper cards and pictograms of the reverted PR #64**, which the brief
  ruled out as a reference.
